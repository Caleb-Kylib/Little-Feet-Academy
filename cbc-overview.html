The Competency-Based Curriculum (CBC) organizes schooling around what learners can do with knowledge—not only what they can recall on one exam. This article outlines how CBC is structured and why it matters for children across Kenya.

What is CBC?

CBC focuses on competencies: combinations of knowledge, skills, and attitudes that learners develop stage by stage. Lessons emphasize inquiry, collaboration, and connecting classroom learning to real life—from early years through junior secondary pathways.

How learning is organized

Key ideas families hear often include:

  • Learning areas — subjects and strands grouped to build coherent experiences.
  • Learner-centred methods — discussion, projects, and practical tasks alongside guided instruction.
  • Continuous assessment — frequent checks that inform teaching, not only end-of-term grades.

Early years and foundation

In lower grades, emphasis is on literacy, numeracy, exploration, and social-emotional growth. Play, storytelling, and structured routines help children build confidence before formal examinations become a central focus.

What parents notice

You may see more project work, portfolios, and rubrics describing “what good looks like.” That reflects CBC’s aim: transparent expectations and support until each learner demonstrates understanding—not a single high-stakes snapshot.
